# 02 · Strategy: decide what the page is for

Run this before the angle, before the structure, before a single line of HTML. It takes about ten
minutes and it is the part of the work that cannot be automated: a pretty site with the wrong
conversion objective is more expensive than a late one, because it burns the pitch and cannot be fixed
by redeploying.

Nothing here is asked if it can be deduced from the evidence (reviews, captions, packaging, press, the
"about" page). What cannot be deduced is written down as a question for the client, never invented.

---

## The five questions, in order

### 1 · What do they really sell? (3 minutes)

Not what they list. The change of situation the customer is buying.

| Evidence | What to read |
|---|---|
| Google reviews | **First.** Customers describe the work in their own words. One-star reviews give you the pain |
| Instagram captions | How the owner describes the business when nobody corrects them |
| Packaging, flyers, quotes | The best input there is: copy written by the owner |
| Press | Usually the origin story and the "why" |
| Facebook "about" | Years of experience, and often a different description than Instagram's |

```
WHAT THEY SELL (product)   :
WHAT PEOPLE BUY (the job)  :
WHAT THEY COMPETE AGAINST  :
```

**Check:** if line 2 equals line 1, it is not done. If line 2 could be printed on a competitor's site
by swapping the logo, it is not done either.

**The real alternative is rarely another company.** In our market it is usually: doing nothing, the
brother-in-law who does it cheap, buying it across the border, or the informal seller on Marketplace. A
page built to beat a formal competitor when the real rival is the brother-in-law argues the wrong case.

### 2 · Who pays, who decides, who uses? (2 minutes)

They are rarely the same person.

```
WHO USES      :
WHO DECIDES   :  (and what they are afraid of)
WHO PAYS      :  (do they need an invoice?)
TICKET / CYCLE / RISK :  low|medium|high / today|weeks|months / low|medium|high
WHERE THE MONEY COMES FROM :  which line of revenue pays the rent
```

**The page is written for the one who decides, illustrated for the one who uses, and made payable for
the one who pays.**

**The forward test**, mandatory for any B2B or institutional lead: if someone opens this link cold, with
no context, do they know in 30 seconds who these people are and whether to hire them? That is how the
page will be used: pasted into a group chat.

**Common trap:** the business shows its flashiest line (the Instagram feed) while a quieter line pays
the bills. When the feed and the reviews do not match, ask.

### 3 · How much does the buyer know? (2 minutes)

This decides **the length of the page**, the biggest structural decision.

| Signal in the research | Awareness | Page |
|---|---|---|
| The messages are "how much is it" | Most aware | Short. Offer, price, one button |
| People search the category on Maps | Solution aware | Medium. Compare and prove |
| The category barely gets searched but the need exists | Problem aware or unaware | Long. It has to teach |
| Only referrals come in | Product aware | Short. Confirm what they were told |

**Sophistication:** most of our market sits at level 1 or 2, where a concrete, verifiable fact still
wins and almost nobody states one. Gyms, beauty, courses and agencies are already at level 3 or more.
**Do not import level-4 mechanics into a level-1 market.**

### 4 · What is the conversion objective? (3 minutes) ← the heart of it

**"That they contact us" is not an objective.** It is a mechanism, and it is the default we fall into
when we skip the analysis. It does not say who, with what, or what happens to the visitor who will not
buy today.

> **Definition:** the conversion objective is the **first irreversible commitment** a visitor can make
> from a screen, in the direction of that business's real money.

Pick **one primary** and at most **one secondary**. Three primaries is none.

| | Objective | The visitor... | The page must carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Qualified message | sends a message that already contains what is needed to quote | finished work with context, process with lead time, a price range or its variables, **WhatsApp with the message pre-written** |
| **B** | Store visit | decides to go, or calls | true hours, map, a verbal reference of how to get there, what it looks like inside, a phone someone answers. **Short page** |
| **C** | Booking | takes a slot from the calendar | real availability, what the first session is like, its price, the cancellation policy |
| **D** | Order | places an order | prices, stock, delivery and lead time, payment methods. **In our market this is an order by WhatsApp, not a cart** |
| **E** | Verified credential | someone with authority reads it cold, believes they are real, and forwards it | names and faces, track record with dates and sources, linked press, institutional clients, and **something downloadable to forward** |
| **F** | Staying in memory | will not buy today, and the page must still be there when they do | one memorable thing, the category said plainly, a cheap way to keep the thread open |
| **G** | Recruiting and suppliers | applies for a job, or offers to distribute | what working there is like, how they pay, an easy path to apply |

**Quick choice:**

| If... | Primary |
|---|---|
| They quote and the ticket is medium | **A** |
| The purchase happens in a physical place, today | **B** |
| They sell time slots | **C** |
| There are prices, stock and repeat purchases | **D** |
| An institution decides, or the page will be forwarded to strangers | **E** |
| The buying cycle is years | **F** |
| The bottleneck they named is people or partners | **G** |

Common real combinations: **A+F** in construction and trades · **B+D** in retail and food · **E+F** in
institutions, professionals and the arts · **C+B** with a calendar and a premises.

**The objective decides three things in the build:** the length of the page, which CTA and where, and
**which event gets instrumented**. Instrument it when building, not after: if the objective is E and we
only measure WhatsApp clicks, we will report a failure that did not happen.

#### When the client says "I just want presence"

**Presence is a state, not an objective.** It is what you have; the objective is what presence buys you.
They are almost always describing **E** or **F** without the vocabulary:

- **E:** "I want to stop being unverifiable". Someone searched them, found a 2019 Facebook page and left.
- **F:** "I want them to remember us". They know the sale is not today.

**The question that turns it into a measurable objective**, for the video or the first call:

> *"If three months from now this worked, what exactly happened? Who wrote to you, or what reached you,
> that does not reach you today?"*

The answer is always concrete, and that answer is the objective.

**And beautiful photos are not decoration in these cases: they are the proof.** In art, food and
hospitality the photograph is the evidence that the thing exists and is good. It goes where the proof
goes, not where the ornament goes.

### 5 · Why does nobody believe them today? (2 minutes)

Credibility is judged **by looking before reading** (Stanford's web credibility research, three years and
more than 4,500 participants, found the look of a site is what people comment on first). A business with 20
real years and a 2016 website is being judged by the website.

The local proof stack, strongest first:

1. Photos of finished work with context. Not stock, not renders, not the workshop.
2. Google reviews quoted with name and date.
3. Named clients or institutions.
4. Press **with a link** (it can be checked, that is the point).
5. Faces and names of the people who do the work.
6. A physical address with a map and a photo of the front door.
7. Years, with the founding year, not "extensive experience".
8. Real certifications and registrations.
9. Numbers, as long as they come from the client and can be defended.

```
WHY NOBODY BELIEVES THEM TODAY :
PROOF AVAILABLE                :
PROOF MISSING                  :  (this goes to the questions for the client)
WHAT GOOGLE SHOWS FOR THEIR NAME TODAY :
```

**Nothing here is published unless it came from a client source.** A number you cannot defend is worse
than no number.

## The block that goes into the dossier

Placed above the sales angle. It is the first thing the person recording the video reads, and the first
third of the video comes from it.

```markdown
## Strategic analysis

**What they sell:** <product> · **What people buy:** <the job> · **Competes against:** <the real alternative>

**Who decides:** <person and their fear> · **Who pays:** <and whether they need an invoice> · **Who uses:** <>
**Ticket / cycle / risk:** <> / <> / <>

**Awareness:** <stage> → page <short|medium|long>
**Sophistication:** <1-5>

**🎯 Primary conversion objective:** <A-G> · <the action, named, with who performs it>
**Secondary:** <A-G or none>
**Measured with:** <the event we will instrument>

**The page DOES carry:** <3 to 5 things>
**The page does NOT carry:** <what the default would have added and is out of place here>

**Why nobody believes them today:** <the credibility gap>
**To confirm with the client:** <list>
```

---

## The three mistakes this exists to prevent

1. **Writing to the audience when an institution pays.** A dance company, a supplier, a professional
   firm: the audience never visits the site; whoever hires them does.
2. **Putting "ask for your free quote" on a high-risk purchase.** When the person cannot decide alone,
   the right ask is smaller: look at the case, download the folder.
3. **Measuring an E or F objective as if it were A**, and declaring a site a failure after two months
   while it is doing exactly its job.

## Reference case: a folk dance company with a world title

| | |
|---|---|
| What they sell | Not dance. A finished cultural act that turns an event into an occasion, with a world title and an international tour behind it |
| Who decides | Event organisers, cultural institutions, embassies, companies, schools |
| Who pays | An institution or a company, which will need a formal quote |
| Awareness | Solution aware: they know a dance company is an option, they cannot verify any |
| The blocker | Not price. They cannot be verified: zero links in the bio, no site, and a shop with the same name outranks them |
| **Primary** | **E · Verified credential** |
| **Secondary** | **F · Staying in memory** (bookings are seasonal and institutional agendas run months ahead) |
| The page carries | Dated, linked press, the world title stated precisely, the tour, formation sizes and repertoire, photography as proof, a press kit in English |
| The page does not carry | A "message us on WhatsApp" CTA aimed at the public. The public never visits |

The agency owner's instinct ("their objective is presence and showing beautiful photos") was pointed in
the right direction and one level short. Presence is the mechanism. **The objective is to be hireable by
someone who has never seen them**, and that changes what goes on the page.

## The headline follows from this

Once the objective and the angle exist, the H1 is written with one formula: **what they sell + the thing
nobody else can say, as a statement**. Finding that thing is three questions: what problem do they solve
(not the category), are they original or verifiable (look for the proof on their own packaging), and do
they produce it themselves, pack it to their own standard, or resell a sealed product. Between two true
USPs the concrete one wins. Details in [13-copy-and-content.md](13-copy-and-content.md).
